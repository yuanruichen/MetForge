# Adaptive research interview

Read this guide for exploratory or resumed planning. Choose questions that change the research design; do not ask every example or enforce a fixed number of rounds.

## Recover the starting point

Start from the user's notes and project documents. Identify the question already stated, decisions already adopted, actual evidence supplied, and the next unresolved choice. If an existing plan is clear, ask about its weakest assumption or present the requested revision instead of repeating intake.

For an idea with no materials, begin with the phenomenon or uncertainty the researcher wants to explain. Do not require results, a paper thesis, a target journal, or an originality claim before research can be planned.

## Questions by decision

| Decision | Useful question | What to preserve |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Scientific question | What exactly is uncertain: existence, location, variability, response, or mechanism? | A question with an object, scope and contrast; exclusions when needed. |
| Motivation | What does the existing work establish, and what does it leave unresolved for this question? | Cited findings separately from the user's interpretation; source/access gaps. |
| Hypotheses | What other explanation could produce the same pattern? Which observation would distinguish it? | Stable H1/H2 IDs, predicted signatures, possible counterevidence, current status. Exploratory questions are allowed without fixed hypotheses. |
| Available evidence | What materials actually exist, and which availability claims are still assumptions? | Available / user-reported / not yet inspected / missing; no invented model, variable, period, or sample count. |
| Technical route | What is the smallest comparison that could weaken one explanation? | Contrasts, necessary variables/resolution, critical settings and limitations. |
| Acceptance | What must be checked before this milestone can be considered usable, and what outcome would change the plan? | Deliverable, validation, uncertainty, and a revision/stop criterion; a null result can still be informative. |
| Synthesis | Does this core represent the question and approach you intend, including these open choices? | User-endorsed plan or explicit draft status; agreement is not evidence for a hypothesis. |

Acceptance criteria concern data quality and the ability to distinguish explanations, not obtaining the researcher's preferred answer. Avoid arbitrary thresholds or deadlines; propose a justification or leave them as decisions to be made before the relevant comparison.

## Two kinds of uncertainty

- **Changes the current decision:** unclear research object, incompatible definitions, conflicting scientific claims, or insufficient evidence to choose a method. Ask about it now.
- **Can be investigated in the plan:** unverified data availability, undecided downstream parameters, or uncertainty requiring a pilot. Put it in a feasibility milestone with an explicit next action; do not prolong the interview until it is solved.

After each material answer, a useful short response is: decision understood, remaining uncertainty, next question. Keep a small evolving outline rather than regenerating a long document every turn. Use named milestones only when they help the project.

## Check the evidence boundary

For a user-reported result, record who reported it and what remains unchecked. For a local figure, distinguish inspecting its appearance from validating the calculation. For a literature claim, distinguish a verified source finding from its applicability to this project. For an unrun experiment, use “proposed comparison” or “expected signature,” never “we found.”

For example, “the moisture budget residual is large” is a reported or verified diagnostic observation depending on its provenance. “Transient eddies dominate” is an interpretation that requires additional support. Ask about omitted terms, temporal resolution, closure, and alternative explanations before updating a hypothesis's evidence status.

## Resuming and saving

On resume, read the core's date/status/latest-log pointer and pending questions, then the relevant recent log entries. Briefly recover the last scientific decision and continue from the next open issue. If documents disagree, identify the discrepancy and use traceable evidence or ask a focused question; do not silently choose the more convenient version.

At a substantive checkpoint, append a decision/evidence entry and update the current core. A change of research direction is a decision worth recording even without a new experiment; label it “no new experimental evidence.” A formatting change is not.

## Design reference

The interview-first approach was informed by the ARS-Codex [plan-mode protocol](https://github.com/Imbad0202/academic-research-skills-codex/blob/main/skills/academic-research-suite/ars/academic-paper/references/plan_mode_protocol.md). That workflow plans manuscript chapters. This independently written skill develops research questions and living project documents, without requiring ARS, its agents, or its runtime. No upstream prompts or scripts are bundled.
